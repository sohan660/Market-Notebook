About the creator

Hi, I'm Sohan Shrestha.

I'm a first-year student at Franklin & Marshall College who is interested in finance, business strategy, and the way good research can make complicated decisions easier to understand.

A little about me

Learning by building things.

I’m exploring Business, Organizations & Society and economics at Franklin & Marshall, with a growing interest in investing and wealth management. I’m still early in college, so I don't pretend to have every answer. I like taking a topic I’m curious about, doing the research, and turning it into something useful.

That approach is why I made this site. A lot of finance information online jumps straight into stock picks, flashy screenshots, or promises about getting rich. I wanted a simpler starting point that a fellow student could actually read and learn from.

My interests include understanding businesses, financial markets, data and analysis, and how people make long-term decisions with their money. I’m especially curious about careers that combine those skills with helping others, such as wealth management.

LaGuardia Community College — Business Services

Researched New York State electrical licensing requirements and created an interactive PDF guide that allows readers to select locations on a map and find relevant licensing information.

Because rules can differ by municipality, the project emphasized checking local requirements and clearly identifying cases where readers should contact the appropriate authority. It taught me how important accuracy and usability are when presenting research.

The Starting Line — Beginner Investment Guide

Created this educational website to explain stocks, ETFs, compounding, investing risk, and ideas from well-known investors in straightforward language. The project includes source links, interactive examples, and clear notes about the limits of historical data.

Why the website exists

Helping people ask better questions.

I'm interested in how finance connects to everyday decisions. I also think a good explanation can be just as useful as a complicated spreadsheet. My goal for this guide isn't to tell anyone what stocks to buy. It's to help readers understand the questions they should be asking before making financial decisions.

What you'll find here

Plain-English lessons, source-backed case studies, and hypothetical calculators that show how investing math works.

What you won't find

Personalized portfolio recommendations, promises of returns, paid tips, or claims that one famous investor has a magic formula.
